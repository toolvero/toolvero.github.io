IPv6 address notation

IPv6 addresses contain eight hexadecimal groups. Leading zeroes may be removed and the longest consecutive run of zero groups may be represented once with ::. Expanded notation is useful when comparing or documenting addresses consistently.

How does IPv6 compression work?

IPv6 addresses contain eight groups of hexadecimal values. Leading zeros in a group can be removed, and one consecutive sequence of zero groups can be represented with a double colon. Expansion restores the full eight-group representation.

Common uses

  • Normalize IPv6 addresses for documentation.
  • Expand compressed notation while troubleshooting.
  • Check how zero compression changes an address without changing its value.
